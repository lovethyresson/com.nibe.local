Autopilot for hot water

Autopilot learns when your household uses hot water, then heats it in the cheapest hours before that. There's no schedule to set.

Hot water runs down with the evening showers, gets heated around midnight when prices are lowest, and is ready for the morning.

How Autopilot picks when to heat

Every day it works out when heating costs the least for the hot water you'll use, and plans again every 15 minutes when prices, sun or your use change.

Temperature

Hotter water costs more per degree and loses more heat while it waits. Above about 58 °C it needs the immersion heater.

So the plan changes from day to day:

A working day, cheap night

Heating at 02:00 to 56 °C lasts the day.

A sunny Saturday

A little at night for the morning, the rest at noon on your own solar power.

A price dip at 14:00

The night heating stays cooler. A top-up in the dip covers the evening.

An unplanned bath

Hot water runs lower than expected, so the next heating moves earlier.

Legionella

Your heat pump normally heats hot water extra hot every few days to kill legionella bacteria. While Autopilot runs live, it turns that periodic hot water off and does the run itself, in the cheapest two hours before it's due.

How hot?

You pick it in Hot water setup: your heat pump's own setting, 65 °C (recommended), or your own from 55 to 70 °C.

How often?

As often as your heat pump's own setting, or every 14 days if it can't be read. Heating that reaches the temperature anyway counts, and the count starts again.

What if the water doesn't get that hot?

Autopilot runs it once each time it's due. If the water still isn't that hot a day after it's due, your heat pump's own periodic hot water comes back on and you get a notice. Autopilot turns it off again once the water reaches the temperature.

What happens when I turn Autopilot off?

Periodic hot water goes back to how you had it.

Good to know

Can my heat pump still heat hot water on its own?

Yes. If hot water gets too cold, your heat pump heats it itself, exactly as it would without Autopilot.

Can I stop heating halfway?

Not really. Once heating starts, it runs to the planned temperature.

How often does it start heating?

At most three times a day, so the compressor doesn't start more than it needs to.

Going on holiday?

Away is for a week or more. Set the day type to Away before you leave. Autopilot stops planning for showers and only keeps the minimum warm until you're back.

I have Flows that turn on More hot water.

They'll heat hot water outside Autopilot's plan. Setup lists any it finds, so you can turn them off or keep them for notifications.

How are savings counted?

Hot water has no learning phase, so savings count from Autopilot's first whole day. The starting point is how your heat pump heated hot water before Autopilot, from up to two weeks of earlier readings in Homey: the electricity per litre and how it paid compared with each day's average price. If Homey has none, for example right after pairing, the day's average price stands in. Each day, the hot water you used is priced that way, and the saving is that minus what Autopilot actually paid. Whole days only. The widget shows this year so far, and the rest of the year at the same pace. More in How savings are counted.

Where's the plan for today?

In your Homey Timeline each morning, and in the Autopilot widget, which shows prices and planned heating at a glance.
