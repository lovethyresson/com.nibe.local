The Hot water device tells you how much shower is left in your tank, and lets you ask for more when you need it. No more guessing from temperatures.

Litres you can shower in.

Hot water available counts litres of 40 °C water, the same measure Nibe prints on its own tank datasheets.

Not how full. How much shower.

Your tank is always full of water. What changes is how hot it is. Hot water comes out the top and cold comes in at the bottom, so the figure falls as you use it, and climbs again while your heat pump heats.

Incoming water, learned.

Colder mains water means fewer litres of shower. Nibe Live works it out from the lowest temperature the bottom of your tank reaches after big draws, over the past month. It stays blank until it knows, and the estimate assumes 10 °C until then.

Need more? Ask for it.

Turn on More hot water for a boost. One-time increase lets you choose how long it lasts, from 2 to 48 hours.

Hotter than you set.

A boost doesn't use your everyday stop temperature. It moves your heat pump up to Large for a while and heats to Hot water stop (Large), even if you normally run Small or Medium. That's the setting to change if boosts run too hot.

68 °C

A real boost on a heat pump set to Small, with Large at 68 °C.

Or short of the mark.

The compressor on its own tops out around 55–60 °C in the tank, a bit less when its heat source is cold. Above that, the immersion heater has to help, and two things commonly stop it.

Operating mode on Manual

On the Main device. In Auto, your heat pump decides for itself and ignores the Allow immersion heater switch.

No schedule blocking it

Your heat pump's schedules can block additional heat. Nibe Live can't see that switch, so check it on the display.

Cancel stops the ask. Not the run.

Turning More hot water off cancels the request straight away. If the compressor has already started, your heat pump finishes that run first. That's normal protection for the compressor, not a fault.

Want hot water heated in the cheapest hours instead? That's Autopilot for hot water.

Good to know.

It says 0 L, but the tank isn't empty.

That's right, oddly enough. Hot water available only counts water you could still shower in at 40 °C. Once the hottest water in the tank is below that, there's none left, even though the tank is full of lukewarm water that's fine for the dishes.

Why does it show more litres than my tank holds?

Because hot water gets mixed with cold at the tap. A tank at 55 °C makes a lot more 40 °C water than its own size. It's counting showers, not filling.

Hot water available is missing.

No tank has been chosen yet. Open Repair on the Hot water device and pick yours in Hot water setup.

It's there, but blank.

Your heat pump hasn't reported both tank temperatures yet. It fills in on the next reading.

It barely moves while I shower.

Two sensors aren't many for a whole tank, so the figure lags a shower in progress and catches up after. It always moves the right way, which is what a Flow needs. Treat it as a good guide, not a meter.

Can a Flow warn me when it's running low?

Yes. Use Hot water available dropped below as a trigger, or Hot water available is less than as a condition.

More hot water won't turn on.

Check Allow hot water. When it's off, your heat pump refuses boosts too. If you only want to stop automatic heating, set a very low start temperature instead, and More hot water keeps working.

Will this match the hot water figure in myUplink?

No. Nibe shows hot water in minutes, on some models only, and doesn't publish how it works it out. Nibe Live's figure is in litres and is worked out here, so the two won't line up.
